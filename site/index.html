How to play

A familiar game with a verifiable loop.

One player spots something. Everyone else follows the clues, explores the area, and captures their own evidence.

  1. 01
    ◎

    Spot something

    Take a photo of an interesting object, landmark, pattern, or local detail.

  2. 02
    ✦

    Build the clue

    Use AI-assisted labels and adjustable image obfuscation to set the challenge difficulty.

  3. 03
    ⌖

    Share the hunt

    Publish to friends, teams, nearby players, or a location-bound game mode.

  4. 04
    ◉

    Capture a match

    Guessers photograph what they found. Image embeddings compare their evidence with the original.

  5. 05
    ↗

    Earn and continue

    Correct matches contribute to points, streaks, achievements, and local leaderboards.

Product goals

Technology that gets out of the way.

The project is designed around a social outdoor game first. The ML, backend, and platform architecture exist to make that loop fair, accessible, resilient, and fun.

01

Encourage active exploration

Create a reason to walk a block farther, notice a hidden detail, or share a place with someone else.

02

Make challenges easy to create

Generate useful clues and difficulty controls from a camera capture without requiring content-authoring expertise.

03

Verify without spoiling

Use similarity scoring as a referee while keeping the answer concealed until the round is resolved.

04

Respect constrained devices

Support optional model delivery, offline capture, fail-closed readiness, and platform-specific resource limits.

05

Share most code, not every compromise

Use Kotlin Multiplatform for domain and UI foundations while retaining native seams for camera, networking, storage, and model delivery.

06

Build toward safe community play

Keep location, account, moderation, privacy, and abuse controls explicit as social modes mature.

Architecture

Shared game logic with explicit platform boundaries.

Eyespie uses a layered Kotlin Multiplatform architecture. Shared code owns product behaviour; native adapters own capabilities that differ materially across Android and iOS.

Design boundaries

Fail closed at capability edges.

  • Downloaded model artifacts are not considered ready until verification succeeds.
  • Cancellation and partial downloads restore capability-derived state rather than assuming success.
  • Platform adapters expose typed outcomes instead of leaking native implementation details into gameplay.
  • Optional assets can be delivered independently from the base application package.

Engineering principles

Built for the conditions of a real mobile game.

Offline-aware

Capture and local state should survive weak connectivity, with explicit synchronization when service returns.

Privacy-conscious

Prefer local inference for visual analysis and minimize the movement of sensitive image and location data.

Observable

Model readiness, transfers, verification failures, and gameplay effects need diagnosable state—not silent fallbacks.

Portable, not lowest-common-denominator

Share durable product logic while using platform-native APIs where lifecycle and security semantics differ.
